Clinical & Treatment

Nephrology & oncology questions.

Common questions about diagnosis, treatment, and ongoing care across our kidney and cancer services.

Nephrology — Kidney Care

What is chronic kidney disease (CKD)?
CKD is a gradual, long-term loss of kidney function. With early diagnosis and stage-wise treatment, its progression can be slowed and complications reduced.
What are the early warning signs of kidney disease?
Early kidney disease is often silent. Possible signs include swelling in the legs or face, changes in urination, fatigue, high blood pressure, and abnormal blood or urine tests.
How is kidney disease diagnosed?
Diagnosis is based on blood tests (such as creatinine and eGFR), urine tests, blood pressure assessment, imaging, and, when required, a kidney biopsy.
Can chronic kidney disease be reversed?
Most chronic kidney damage cannot be reversed, but the right treatment, diet, and control of blood pressure and sugar can slow progression and protect remaining function.
What is acute kidney injury (AKI)?
AKI is a sudden drop in kidney function, often reversible if diagnosed early. Prompt evaluation and treatment of the cause help protect the kidneys.
Do you provide dialysis support?
Yes. The clinic offers outpatient Hemodialysis (OPD) support and Peritoneal Dialysis (CAPD) guidance, including training, review, and ongoing monitoring.
What is the difference between hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis?
Hemodialysis filters blood through a machine, usually at a centre. Peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) uses the lining of the abdomen and can be done at home. The doctor helps choose what suits you.
When is a kidney transplant needed?
A transplant may be considered in advanced kidney failure. The clinic supports pre-transplant assessment and structured post-transplant follow-up to protect the new kidney.
What are nephrotic and nephritic syndromes?
These are groups of kidney conditions involving protein or blood loss in the urine, such as Lupus Nephritis and IgA Nephropathy. They are evaluated and treated based on biopsy and lab findings.
Can diabetes and high blood pressure affect my kidneys?
Yes. Diabetes and high blood pressure are leading causes of kidney disease. Early control of both is one of the most effective ways to protect kidney health.
Do you treat kidney stones and urinary infections?
Yes. The clinic manages kidney stone disease with prevention strategies, and treats recurrent, persistent, or complicated urinary tract infections.
Is special kidney care available during pregnancy?
Yes. Obstetric nephrology provides specialised support for pregnant women living with kidney disease or pregnancy-related kidney problems, balancing maternal and kidney health.
What is onco-nephrology?
Onco-nephrology manages kidney complications linked to cancer and its treatment, such as chemotherapy-related kidney injury and electrolyte disorders — a key strength of this dual-speciality clinic.

Oncology — Cancer Care

What cancer care does the clinic provide?
Care spans cancer evaluation and diagnosis, systemic therapy, multidisciplinary coordination, supportive care, survivorship follow-up, palliative care, and counselling.
How is cancer diagnosed and staged?
Diagnosis combines clinical assessment, pathology interpretation, imaging review, and staging. Accurate staging forms the foundation for an individualised treatment plan.
What is systemic cancer therapy?
Systemic therapy treats cancer throughout the body and includes chemotherapy, targeted therapy, hormonal therapy, and immunotherapy, chosen based on the diagnosis and stage.
What are targeted therapy and immunotherapy?
Targeted therapy acts on specific features of cancer cells, while immunotherapy helps the body's immune system fight cancer. Both are planned when clinically appropriate.
How are chemotherapy side effects managed?
Supportive care manages fatigue, nausea, infection risk, anaemia, and nutrition-related effects, helping patients tolerate treatment and feel better through it.
What is multidisciplinary cancer care?
It is coordinated care involving surgeons, radiation oncologists, radiologists, and pathologists, so every treatment decision reflects a shared, expert-reviewed approach.
What is cancer survivorship care?
Survivorship care is structured follow-up after treatment. It watches for recurrence, manages long-term effects, and keeps quality of life at the centre.
What is palliative care and when is it needed?
Palliative care focuses on comfort, symptom relief, and emotional support, especially in advanced illness. It can be provided alongside treatment, with decisions shared openly with families.
Can cancer treatment affect my kidneys?
Yes. Some cancer treatments can affect kidney function. The clinic's onco-nephrology support monitors and manages this so therapy can continue safely.
Do you offer counselling for patients and families?
Yes. Patient and family counselling provides clear, compassionate explanation of the diagnosis, treatment choices, expected outcomes, and prognosis to support informed decisions.
Are vaccinations and genetic counselling available?
Yes. Preventive cancer vaccinations and hereditary-risk (genetic) counselling are offered when clinically relevant.
How soon should I see an oncologist after a diagnosis?
It is best to consult as early as possible. Timely evaluation allows accurate staging and a prompt, well-planned start to the most suitable treatment.
